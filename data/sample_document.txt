Artificial intelligence is changing the way people live and work in India. Over the last decade, the country has seen rapid growth in digital services, and companies such as Google, Microsoft and Infosys have opened large research centres in Bengaluru and Hyderabad. The government of India launched the National Strategy for Artificial Intelligence in 2018 to encourage innovation in healthcare, agriculture, education and smart cities. Farmers now use mobile applications that predict rainfall and suggest the best time for sowing crops. Hospitals use machine learning models to detect diseases such as diabetes and cancer from medical images, which helps doctors to treat patients earlier.

Universities are also updating their courses. Institutes such as the Noida Institute of Engineering and Technology in Greater Noida teach natural language processing, deep learning and data science to thousands of students every year. Students build chatbots, translate languages and summarize long documents using modern transformer models. Experts believe that language technology will be especially important in India because the country has more than twenty official languages, and millions of people prefer to use the internet in their own mother tongue.

However, there are challenges. Many regional languages do not have enough digital text to train large models, and concerns about privacy, bias and job losses are growing. Researchers say that responsible development, open datasets and skilled engineers are essential. If these challenges are handled carefully, artificial intelligence could add nearly five hundred billion dollars to the Indian economy by 2025, according to a report published by NITI Aayog.
